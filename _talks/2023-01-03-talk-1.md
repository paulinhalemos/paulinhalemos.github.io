Standalone Meeting of the American Naturalist, 2023

Talking Science Together

**Authors** Paula Lemos da Costa, Pablo Lechón-Alonzo, Zachary Miller, and Stefano Allesina

**Abstract** Ecological communities are highly diverse and understanding coexistence is a central goal in
ecology. Niche partitioning and variation in resource use are mechanisms that promote
coexistence by reducing niche overlap and creating asymmetries in resource use. Different
strategies for resource acquisition are hypothesized to be constrained by trade-offs, with the
strength and shape of the trade-off influencing which strategy is favored. We used a classic
consumer-resource model to study diverse communities of consumers subject to trade-offs in
resource use. For a wide range of trade-off strengths, we show ample coexistence between
species with final community richness highly influenced by trade-off strength. In particular,
weak trade-offs, favoring generalist strategies, typically lead to richer communities. Surprisingly,
however, we also prove the existence of a doomsday species-- a perfect competitor that drives all
other species to extinction. Communities with strong trade-offs favoring specialists are typically
less diverse but protected from this doomsday scenario.
